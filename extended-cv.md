## About

I lead programs at NASA Headquarters that turn satellite and airborne
observations into trusted, openly available data, and I work to get
those data into the hands of people managing fish, wildlife, fire, and
land. I started as a field biologist and quantitative ecologist, moved
into data science at USGS, and came to NASA through the AAAS Science &
Technology Policy Fellowship.

## Experience

**Program Executive, Earth Science Data Systems (ESDS)**, NASA
Headquarters  
Earth Science Division, Science Mission Directorate \| Washington, DC \|
Nov 2024 – Present

Programmatic lead for NASA’s operational Earth data production
portfolio: Foundational Data Products (FDP), the Fire Information for
Resource Management System (FIRMS), the Land, Atmosphere Near-real-time
Capability for Earth Observations (LANCE), Science Investigator-led
Processing Systems (SIPS), and the Earth Science Information Partners
(ESIP) cooperative agreement. About \$40M per year, executed by civil
servant project teams and their contractors at four NASA centers.

- Leading formulation of Foundational Data Products from concept to a
  leadership-approved framework (definition, designation criteria,
  product specifications, portfolio management); required every product
  to meet NASA Standard Data Product criteria.
- Consolidating existing data production work into an FDP budget more
  than 20% smaller than its current cost; set designation criteria that
  retire duplicative products and centralize processing in the cloud;
  reused NASA’s Senior Review framework for FDP evaluation instead of
  building a new process.
- Brought Headquarters accountability to two long-running near-real-time
  systems (LANCE and FIRMS) that had operated with minimal oversight:
  required their first formal project plans, made Earth Action a
  co-signatory, and required it to fund non-data-system work such as
  science and outreach, cutting ESDS’s share of those costs by about
  half. Now extending the model to NASA Black Marble.
- Over more than a year, moved Goddard’s Earth Sciences Division
  (Code 610) and the ESDIS Project (Code 423) from limited coordination
  to a working partnership on LANCE and FIRMS; the two organizations now
  place staff on each other’s subproject leadership teams.
- Requested a capability to track dependencies among SIPS data products;
  the ESDIS Project grew it into a full knowledge graph and management
  system that now informs continuity risk management and FDP priorities.
- Oversee and direct about 10 civil servants in center project offices,
  who in turn manage contractor teams: set priorities, review
  deliverables, and hold projects to cost and schedule. Selected the FDP
  Project Manager and negotiated the agreement standing up the new FDP
  project office at Goddard.
- Identified the AAAS Science & Technology Policy Fellowship as a new
  way to bring talent into ESDS, now in use; mentor and delegate
  portfolio workstreams to the incoming fellow; set stretch assignments
  for the program’s operations officer; mentor 1-2 NASA interns each
  year.
- Leading the use of data usage metrics across the ESDS portfolio,
  including private-sector use, to guide Earth Science Division
  investment priorities, center-level engagement, and which products FDP
  will designate.
- First program executive to provide direct, consistent SIPS oversight;
  led ESDIS to institute quarterly check-ins with each SIPS.
- Led a FIRMS enhancement through a partnership with Google.org while
  keeping continuity on a colleague’s high-visibility work.
- Scoping a joint NASA-NOAA approach to Level-0 data reprocessing to cut
  duplicate interagency spending.
- Reimagined ESDS working group governance in line with Earth Science to
  Action strategy and agency guidance; charter updates for the ESDSWG,
  DAAC, and LANCE user working groups were widely adopted.
- Headquarters award oversight for the ESIP cooperative agreement
  (deliverables, spend plans, compliance with award terms); started the
  first regular NASA-NOAA-USGS coordination meeting of ESIP agency
  representatives.
- Served on the Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies Data and

## Software

- [`bbsAssistant`](https://github.com/trashbirdecology/bbsAssistant).
  Burnett, J.L., L.S. Wszola, and G. Palomo-Munoz (2019). R package for

- [`distanceTravelled`](https://github.com/trashbirdecology/distancetravelled).
  Burnett, J.L., N.B. Price, and A.J. Tyre. R package for calculating
  the distance traveled by a multispecies community along a time series.
  *Status: stable.*

  Burnett. R package for numerical differentiation of noisy, non-smooth
  data. *Status: stable.*
- [`regimeDetectionMeasures`](https://github.com/trashbirdecology/regimedetectionmeasures).
  Burnett, J.L. and N.B. Price (2019). R package for univariate and
  multivariate regime detection measures on community time series. DOI
  10.5281/zenodo.2547705. *Status: dormant.*
- [`USAvian`](https://github.com/trashbirdecology/USAvian). Burnett,
  J.L., K.R. Burgio, and A. Fournier. Interactive map connecting the
  U.S. bird conservation and management networks. *Status:
  experimental.*
- [`abruptdata`](https://github.com/regime-shifts/abruptdata). Burnett,
  J.L., X. Benito, and K. Braziunas. R package and information
  repository for ecological datasets exhibiting abrupt change. *Status:
  experimental.*
